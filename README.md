# Medicaid Expansion and Hospital Finances: Difference-in-Differences Analysis

## Project Overview

This project examines the impact of the Affordable Care Act's (ACA) Medicaid expansion on hospital finances and healthcare utilization using applied econometric techniques.

The analysis compares hospitals in Medicaid expansion and non-expansion states to evaluate changes in financial performance and patient utilization following the 2014 policy implementation.

This research was completed as part of my graduate studies in Applied Economics at Lehigh University in Fall 2025.

## Research Objectives

- Evaluate the impact of Medicaid expansion on hospital financial performance.
- Examine changes in uncompensated charity care.
- Assess the relationship between insurance coverage expansion and hospital revenues.
- Investigate changes in hospital utilization.
- Evaluate the robustness of estimated treatment effects across alternative econometric specifications.

## Econometric Methodology

The analysis employs several econometric techniques commonly used in causal inference and economic policy evaluation:

- Difference-in-Differences (DiD)
- Parallel Trends Assessment
- Linear Trend Regression
- Event-Study Analysis
- Joint F-Tests
- Regression Models with Hospital-Level Controls
- Two-Way Fixed Effects (TWFE)
- Staggered Treatment Timing

The analysis compares hospitals in expansion and non-expansion states before and after the 2014 Medicaid expansion. Additional specifications incorporate hospital characteristics, hospital fixed effects, year fixed effects, and variation in state-level expansion timing.

## Data

The analysis uses hospital-level panel data covering 2007–2019.

The three primary outcome variables are:

- Charity care per bed
- Revenue per bed
- Patient days per bed

## Key Findings

| Outcome | Baseline DiD Estimate | Two-Way Fixed Effects Estimate |
|---|---:|---:|
| Charity Care per Bed | -$44,474 | -$52,445 |
| Revenue per Bed | +$35,498 | +$26,910 |
| Patient Days per Bed | -4.12 | -3.73 |

- Medicaid expansion was associated with a substantial and statistically significant reduction in charity care per bed.
- Hospital revenues increased relative to non-expansion states, although the estimated effects were not statistically significant.
- No statistically significant changes in hospital utilization were identified.
- The reduction in charity care remained statistically significant across alternative model specifications.
- Accounting for staggered Medicaid expansion timing produced an estimated reduction in charity care of approximately $56,250 per bed.

## Limitations

Joint F-tests of pre-treatment coefficients produced mixed evidence regarding the parallel trends assumption. Statistically significant pre-treatment differences were identified for charity care and patient days per bed.

Consequently, the estimated treatment effects for these outcomes should be interpreted with caution, particularly when drawing causal conclusions.
